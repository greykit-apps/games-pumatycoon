## Design Context

### Users
Two audiences, weighted equally. **Raccoon Tycoon owners** who want a faithful digital table to play solo against bots or hot-seat with friends, and who will judge it against the cardboard. **Newcomers** arriving from greykit.com who have never seen the game and must be able to learn it by playing: the walkthrough, the help and the shape of the screen have to teach, without slowing down the owner who already knows the rules. Played at a desk, in the gaps of a day.

### Brand Personality
Dry, practitioner, brief. greykit.com is "a small arcade of bad ideas" from a security consultant who is "not a developer, allegedly"; the voice is wry and specific, never zany, never emoji. The log reads like a trade blotter: who did what, at what price.

**Emotional goal: a trading terminal.** The market is the game. Prices, moves and bids should be front and centre and feel live: you push a price, you watch it move, you read the board like a ticker. Auctions should feel like a trading-floor moment. The tactical skin is a financial-terminal edge on the board game, not a costume over it.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tactical tribe**, shared with PumaRally and PumaTower: deep navy ground `#05070f`, cyan `#54f2ff` for the live thing, amber `#ffb000` for warnings and the start player, green for money and gains, red for railroads and loss. Dark only. Monospace throughout: here it is the terminal's voice, not a shortcut.
- **No raccoons, no Astoria.** Original vector icons, renamed places and railroads (big-cat lines, Cougar Country). Nothing from the printed artwork. No "cub" in any name.
- **Commodities are told apart by icon shape, never by colour alone**: sheaf, logs, ingot, pile, crate, bottle. Colour reinforces; shape carries.
- **ES modules, no build, no dependencies, no web fonts, offline at play time.**
- **Desktop first.** Judge and design at 1280–1600px; the stacked phone layout must stay usable and must not break, but it is not the target.

### Design Principles
1. **The market is the hero.** The six price tracks and their movement are the first thing the eye reads and the last thing it forgets. Everything else is an order book around them.
2. **Show the move, then the result.** A price rise, a sale, a won auction should be visible as a change, not just a new number.
3. **Faithful before flavoured.** An owner must never re-learn a rule because of the skin. Names are a skin; rule behaviour is exact; gaps are declared, not invented.
4. **Teach in place.** A newcomer should learn from the screen itself: costs, effects and consequences shown where the decision is made, rather than in a manual.
5. **Every live control answers the question being asked.** One place to look for your move; nothing pressable that the engine would refuse.
